Another agent to work with in this chat: invite it instead (POST internal/channels/{id}/members)
  data.parent_id: the session it hangs under on the page (its launcher). Not given: the session your ir runs in, so one you start is your child: listed under you, archived with you. To start one of its own: channel.update data.parent_id:=null after.
  _data.cwd: . is your own folder and a relative path goes from it; ~ and ~/x the home; host:path here; app:path at that app's computer (GET internal/apps), where it then runs; an absolute path as it is.
  guild_id: a folder group's id (guild.list), self yours; not given: its folder's group, yours when yours holds its folder.
  _data.agent: not given: a channel with no agent of its own, listed in _data.cwd; agents join it (POST internal/channels/{id}/members) and people speak in it. Given: claude, codex, pi, dsh or agy (GET internal/agents: those that can start here, their modes); _data.mode: one of them; model, effort, preset: the agent's own (SessionState.models, efforts; GET internal/sessions/{id}/presets).
  _data.role: start it as a kept role (GET internal/roles): its agent, model (dsh: preset) and card; effort and mode are this start's (_data.effort, _data.mode) or the agent's own. _data.nick, _data.persona, _data.avatar: its card from the start (ir api help profile).
  _data.text: its first message, from you: it reads [ir:<your session>] before it. ir prints the id, with text once the agent took it. Started from a session, it joins that session's channel: the text is then said to it there, its answer said there.
  --wait: holds your turn till its first turn ends, then prints its reply: for a session that does a job apart from your channel and reports to you. Not for an agent in your channel (one you invited or started there): its words are said in the channel and its answer to you calls you, so waiting only holds your turn and gets you words everyone has already seen.

# wake
PATCH internal/channels/{id}/members/{member} {nick?, on?} -> ChannelMembers  rename a member (agent:<session>; its nick is the same in every channel), set what wakes an agent (on: the whole list); any member, any agent's, and the channel says so; refused (422 bad_on): a loop of agents waking each other by their last words alone (an agent calls another with @); warn: a ring through a command  [more]
  Every member is given all that is said in the channel when it is next woken; on says what wakes it beside @nick (always). A list, each one of:
  {"message":{"from":[…],"words":[…]}}: from "*" every person, "<app>/<login>", "<app>/*", "agent:<session>" (its turn's last words); none: anyone. words: any of them (case not minded); none: any words.
  {"command":"name"} a person's /name; {"cron":"m h dom mon dow"} the host's time; {"event":["join","leave","rename","wake","error","ask"]} a member came or went, an agent renamed, someone's wake rules changed, an agent's error, an agent asks.
  Each may have "text": what it is to do then, given with what woke it ("greet them"). [] @ only. A new session's own agent: its maker's words. E.g. on:='[{"message":{"from":["*"]}},{"event":["join"],"text":"greet them"}]'.
  Take care: a message entry of no from wakes on every agent's last words too; two agents woken by each other's words go round till paused (30 in a row, or 4 turns of words alone; warned as set). Read GET internal/channels/{id}/members first: set on whole.
GET internal/channels/{id}/scripts - -> Script[]  the commands of the channel (scripts): name, run, on, enabled, who made and last changed it, its last run  view=name,on,enabled,run
POST internal/channels/{id}/scripts {name, run, on, enabled?} -> Script  add a command: a command line run at the host in the channel's folder when its on (as above; text not used) fires; any member; warn: a ring  [more]
  Given the message as JSON on stdin and as IR_TEXT, IR_FROM, IR_ARGS, IR_TRIGGER. What it prints (exit 0) is said by Iridium; @nick in it wakes that agent; ir in it speaks as Iridium too. 60 s at most. To wake an agent on words or a time, give the agent an on, not a command that prints @nick.
